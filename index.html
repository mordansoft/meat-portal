Curing salt and nitrite, plainly

All questions
01What is curing salt?Curing salt is ordinary salt with a small, exactly known share of sodium nitrite mixed in. The salt is there to dilute the nitrite so that a home cook can weigh it. 02What does nitrite do in meat?Three jobs: it holds back the bacteria that cause botulism, it gives cured meat its pink colour, and it gives it the “cured” flavour that keeps instead of turning rancid. 03Is curing salt dangerous? Which doses are toxic?Yes, in two different ways. Straight from the pack it is a poison in amounts that fit in a spoon. In the meat, at the right dose, the concern is long-term, not acute. 04Which documents the calculators are built onEvery limit in SaltCure comes from a published US federal rule for meat plants. Those rules do not regulate home kitchens — we use them because they are the clearest public numbers there are, and the alternative is a forum post.
